# Takeoff Market Validation Research Plan

## Main Research Question

Does the Takeoff proposal (embedded commerce cards for renovation purchases inside AI agents) align with real pain points and workflows that property owners and building material suppliers actually experience?

## Proposal Requirements to Validate

**From plumb-takeoff-reframe.md:**

1. **Quantity takeoff with waste factor** — Converting room dimensions → order with visible waste, not guessed
2. **Sample ordering** — Request sample before ordering full quantity
3. **Lead-time vs. site schedule** — Traffic-light check of delivery against contractor timeline
4. **Substitution handling** — Out-of-stock alternatives with rule re-checks (drop, clearance, etc.)
5. **Multi-party approval** — Client sign-off, contractor approval, separate payer
6. **Delivery logistics** — Site access, floor level, receiving coordination
7. **Agent-legible specs** — Empty fields spoken, not guessed; one record serves homeowner, trade, agent
8. **Order thread continuity** — All decisions in one attributed timeline across suppliers

## Research Subtopics

### Subtopic 1: Renovation Software User Experiences (Forums & Reviews)

What do property owners actually struggle with when using renovation project management or procurement software?

**Expected findings:**
- Common painpoints with quantity estimation, ordering, scheduling
- How homeowners currently make substitute decisions
- Multi-party approval bottlenecks
- Lead-time surprises and how they're handled
- Whether chat/agent-based procurement is emerging

**Sources:** Reddit r/Renovation, r/HomeImprovement, Houzz forums, renovation software user reviews

---

### Subtopic 2: Property Owner + Contractor Renovation Workflows

What is the current friction in how homeowners, contractors, and suppliers interact during renovations?

**Expected findings:**
- Information loss between parties
- Approval delays and what causes them
- Quantity mistakes and returns
- How decisions are currently documented
- Whether existing agents (ChatGPT, etc.) are already being used for renovation planning

**Sources:** Renovation forums, contractor review sites, homeowner blogs, Reddit threads

---

### Subtopic 3: Building Material Supplier Sales & Operations

What do tile sellers, fixture suppliers, and builders' merchants actually need from buyers to reduce returns and support sales?

**Expected findings:**
- Why returns happen (quantity, lead-time, specs)
- How samples are currently ordered and used
- Approval workflows (who decides, who pays)
- Whether suppliers already have "smart order" tools
- Current chat/agent adoption among commercial customers

**Sources:** Ecommerce platform forums, construction software reviews, supplier interviews (if available), B2B ecommerce case studies

---

## Synthesis Goals

1. **Map Proposal to Real Needs:** Which Takeoff artifacts (takeoff card, sample request, lead-time check, etc.) directly address observed pain points?
2. **Identify Gaps:** Are there renovation workflows Takeoff doesn't cover that real users say are critical?
3. **Supplier Alignment:** Do building material suppliers actually want chat-based ordering, or do they prefer existing channels (email, phone, their own portals)?
4. **Homeowner Readiness:** Are property owners ready to buy materials through AI agents, or is this ahead of market adoption?
5. **Competitive Reality:** Are there existing tools or workflows already solving these problems better?

## Success Criteria

- At least 3 distinct user pain points from research that Takeoff directly addresses
- At least 1 gap identified where current proposal is misaligned with actual user needs
- Clear evidence whether suppliers would prioritize chat-based purchasing vs. other channels
- Assessment of whether embedded agents (Muse, ChatGPT) are realistic venues for renovation commerce
